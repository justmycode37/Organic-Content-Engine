## Applied to Silicon Footnote

Each episode gets **two complete title and cover pairs**. Start with an entity, physical object, or specific human consequence the audience recognizes. Open a curiosity gap using a question, a reversal, or a comparison the episode can pay off. Cover copy has up to four large words and gives the title another angle.

Create original full-bleed art for each pair. One direction can use a close-up or a dramatic hero shot. The other can use scale, an unexpected setting, or a visual metaphor. A cover can depict a concept that never appears in the footage. Clearly fictional or dramatized art should still relate to the topic, and the title should accurately describe the story. Prefer strong silhouettes, deliberate color separation, and a composition that survives a 160 × 90 preview. Remove decorative labels, tiny details, document cards, and generic backgrounds.

For the Mars pilot, one pair uses a low-angle rover and glowing path with **AI ON MARS**. The other uses Earth hanging over a tiny rover with **NO JOYSTICK**. Both make the planning story tangible without using a video frame. The episode explains that Claude planned on Earth and humans reviewed the commands. [NASA route image and context](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mapping-perseverances-route-with-ai/).

The Mac producer plans both pairs and generates two artwork files with Codex's built-in image generation through its signed-in ChatGPT subscription. It adds the exact lettering locally and saves the original art, selected copy, and two 3840 × 2160 JPEGs. View or download them under **Episodes → YouTube titles and thumbnails**. Compare watch-time share when testing complete packages. Hold the title fixed when isolating the thumbnail. Check first-30-second retention alongside clicks when judging whether a package found the right viewers.
