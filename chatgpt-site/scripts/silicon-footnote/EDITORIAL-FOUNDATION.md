# Silicon Footnote long-form editorial foundation

Research snapshot · 24 November 2026 · English-language YouTube · 10:00–15:00

**Channel promise.** One consequential AI or technology story in each episode. Start with the thing that changed, then show the overlooked document, mechanism, or trade-off that explains why it matters. The viewer should leave able to explain the change and its consequences in one sentence.

**Launch direction.** Start with infrastructure, compute, and the economics of AI labs. The sample has strong demand for both a physical explanation and a business reversal. Cover releases, labor, and synthetic media when a new paper, filing, controlled test, or product decision gives the episode a claim to investigate.

**First episode.** Lead with the November 16 hyperscale electricity study. The premise is timely, the measurement method is a story in itself, and the biggest numbers come with an explainable range. Follow with what Anthropic’s IPO prospectus reveals about financing compute. Use the AI chip episode below as the model for how to explain a system, and the BBC paper-led episode as the model for how to set the boundary of a claim.

### What the strongest examples actually do

- **Put a reversal and its evidence close together.** At 0:00, Economy Media opens on a delay/cancellation claim. Around 0:11 it introduces a specific Texas expansion report, then separates cancellations (2:30) and delays (3:10) before exploring the grid (4:00). The thumbnail presents the halting-build idea instantly, but its “50% Being Cancelled” treatment collapses two categories that the opening itself separates. For Silicon Footnote, use a named project and put its actual status in the title, visual and narration. [Opening and chapters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e0Dlr07jfjA&t=11s).
- **Make an invisible system followable.** Silicon Up Close asks the viewer to remember their last AI question in the first seconds. The chapters then move into the building (1:41), chip (3:01), memory (4:25), and transistors (6:46), before following the answer back (11:06). A copper-colored cutaway and the short “Inside One Chip” thumbnail line give the title a visual answer. The description labels the chip interiors as illustrative and says the hardware that answered a specific prompt is unknown. Reuse the structure by following one request, dollar, shipment, or unit of power across a system. [Opening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oBKrR3i6jWM&t=9s) · [Return](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oBKrR3i6jWM&t=666s).
- **Explain a finding via a causal loop.** BBC Global names the research in its opening, then explains the mechanism in everyday terms: workers are also customers, and replacing workers can reduce spending. The next chapters move from mechanism to proposed responses (4:34). Make the study type part of the first explanation. This is a modeled scenario under assumptions about substitution and demand. [Video opening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3gYyCB2n9o) · [Original working paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20617).
- **Give the viewer a place or an object.** CNBC’s opening captions put a reporter among server racks and mention the heat before turning to grid capacity. Its thumbnail shows equipment, and the chapters walk through finding power (1:58), hardening the grid (6:06), cooling (8:45), and work per watt (11:56). Bloomberg’s chapter list offers a parallel structure at human scale: start with one neighbor at 1:08, then move through Virginia land use and opposition to power demand. [CNBC opening and chapters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MJQIQJYxey4) · [Bloomberg resident chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SpMIs6AnUW8&t=68s).
- **Connect a business claim to a testable denominator.** Economy Media’s labor video pairs the promise of cheap automation with a turn to token pricing (3:25), usage (4:10), and enterprise spending (6:20). Its Google-logo thumbnail signals a company story, so a Silicon Footnote version would need documented company-specific evidence for that treatment. The other Economy Media opener puts a laptop price on the screen’s mental canvas within eight seconds. The move makes the question relatable. A causal explanation would also need comparable configurations, dates and other plausible price drivers. [Labor video's pricing chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cfaZZPjA3g0&t=205s) · [Laptop-price opening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R8nAB_oEf_I&t=8s).

## What qualifies as viral material for this channel

A candidate earns a pitch when it has a question a non-specialist would click, evidence to answer it, and enough change across the story to justify 10–15 minutes. Public views indicate demand. A strong opening, useful explanation, and satisfying ending are editorial tests. Evaluate those independently.

| Pitch test | What a strong answer looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Why now? | Name a dated filing, release, study, outage, rule, demonstration, or change in deployment. Tell the audience what changed in a single sentence. |
| Why click? | Pair one recognizable entity or object with one surprising, supportable consequence. The title makes a specific promise, and the thumbnail shows the thing that makes the promise tangible. |
| Why keep watching? | Find three distinct discoveries. Each changes the answer, scale, or consequence. Arrange them as a chain of causes or a sequence of evidence. |
| Why believe it? | Put the first specific exhibit within 30 seconds. Trace the decisive claim to a document, dataset, original recording, or reproducible test. State what the evidence can support. |
| Why send it to someone? | The takeaway is useful beyond the news cycle. A viewer can retell the mechanism or point to the chart without needing the whole setup again. |
| Can we produce it? | Identify credible sources, usable footage, a meaningful original diagram, and a consequence with a named stakeholder. Review the title and thumbnail against the same evidence as the narration. |

**Packaging examples for a power story.** Test “How Researchers Measured America’s Biggest Data Centers” with a cluster of facilities and a map legend, or “How Much Power Do America’s Biggest Data Centers Use?” with one installation and a compact “68–99 TWh” range. Test both at mobile scale. For the second version, deliver the range and sample in the opening and explain why it is a range before asking viewers to interpret it.

### A 12-minute episode outline

| Time | Story work | Silicon Footnote visual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:30 | Open on the change and one piece of evidence. Ask the question the episode will answer. | Source footage or an original labeled illustration, plus one legible line from the document. |
| 0:30–1:30 | Set the date, the old expectation, and who is affected. | Document highlight or short timeline with a source/date. |
| 1:30–4:00 | Explain the first mechanism in ordinary language. | Sequential diagram or labeled terrain, with one object or quantity carried through. |
| 4:00–7:30 | Present the decisive comparison and show how it was measured. | Chart with unit, common baseline where applicable, geography, and denominator. |
| 7:30–10:30 | Test the leading interpretation. Introduce the best limitation or alternative explanation, then show who bears the consequence. | Second source, bounded quote, or verified footage with an identification plate. |
| 10:30–12:00 | Answer the opening question and name one concrete development worth watching. | Return to the opening artifact with a new annotation. |

Use the runtime range as an editing constraint. Let the native Titan recording set the pace, then cut passages that repeat a point. Vary the visual when the thought or evidence changes. Build around the branch's document highlights, sequential diagrams, charts, perspective labels, and footage identification plates. Illustrative visuals should carry that label when they could be mistaken for a documented facility, measurement, or hardware interior. ## Source-backed episode briefs

### 1. How much power do America’s biggest data centers use?

**Priority and timing.** Lead episode. A paper published November 16, 2026, estimated **68–99 TWh** across operating scenarios for **403 U.S. hyperscale data centers** operating between May 2024 and April 2025. The range, sample, geography, and study period should travel together. Connect the national data to an actual balancing area if its underlying records support the example. [Peer-reviewed study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-026-01985-7) · [Harvard research release](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/hyperscale-data-centers-energy-consumption-carbon-emissions-newly-estimated-by-researchers/).

**Cut.** Identify the sample and why power use is hard to observe → show how facilities and operating scenarios were estimated → draw the 68–99 TWh range → explain the difference between capacity in MW and period energy use in TWh → show what a regional grid operator can see that a national sum hides. Berkeley Lab’s national update covers a broader population. Put the definitions beside any comparison. Footage lead: source a facility, transmission or substation shot with a reusable license and label generic footage as illustrative. [Berkeley Lab 2025 update](https://www.energy.gov/documents/united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report-2025-update) · [EIA electricity data](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/).

### 2. What Anthropic’s IPO prospectus reveals about financing AI

**Priority and timing.** Follow-up while the filing is fresh. The November 18 prospectus set **$83/share** for the offering. The cover distinguishes **$81.34B** in initial public offering price in aggregate, before the underwriting discount, from **$78.84B** in proceeds to Anthropic before expenses, with separate proceeds to selling stockholders. The November 20 closing notice records the full option exercise and closing of the concurrent private placement. Date the numbers and name the measure on every graphic. [Final SEC prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) · [Pricing notice](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering) · [Closing notice](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close).

**Cut.** Open the cover page → diagram new-share proceeds, selling stockholders, and private placement → read the filing's “Use of Proceeds” and “Risk Factors” alongside the separately dated compute commitments → explain which promises are contractual, conditional or forward-looking. If including market capitalization, date the price and specify the share-count basis. Use original charts and separately cleared footage. The founder film can inform interview structure. [Prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm) · [Founder film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sof3vW4mHTs).

### 3. Why a faster AI chip still has to wait for memory

**Trigger.** Use a current accelerator deployment, a dated price change, or a reproducible benchmark. Show one workload moving through memory, compute, and interconnect before comparing throughput, latency, or cost per completed request. Keep workload, precision, platform boundary, and baseline legible wherever results appear. A vendor platform claim should carry vendor attribution. This is a reusable assignment pending a fresh trigger. [NVIDIA Rubin platform](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-the-nvidia-rubin-platform-six-new-chips-one-ai-supercomputer/) · [NVIDIA Rubin GPU architecture](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-nvidia-rubin-gpu-architecture-powering-the-era-of-agentic-ai/) · [IEEE device roadmap](https://irds.ieee.org/editions/2022).

**Cut.** One prompt → show a simplified data path → illustrate why reading model weights or cached context can constrain a given workload → compare identical conditions or label where conditions differ → return to which workloads benefit. Build an original, labeled illustrative cutaway with vendor-documented parts. [Silicon Up Close structure reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oBKrR3i6jWM).

### 4. What an AI coding agent actually saves on a real task

**Trigger.** Use a new measured study or one repeatable test paired with a current tool release. METR’s early-2025 randomized study involved 16 experienced developers and 246 tasks in familiar open-source repositories. The observed result was 19% longer with AI allowed in that setting. Its February 2026 update describes why the new experiment design needed changes. Stanford payroll research offers a separate employment indicator, with different data and causal limits. [METR randomized study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089) · [METR design update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) · [Stanford payroll analysis, August 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf).

**Cut.** Show a task and measured completion criterion → compare time spent prompting, inspecting, reworking and validating → distinguish the experiment’s sample from a single recorded task → end with which work got easier and which costs stayed. Log the tool version, task context, timing method, and failure cases. A coding-time result and an employment trend answer different questions. [BBC research-led reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3gYyCB2n9o).

### 5. What a headline AI training cost includes

**Trigger.** Reuse the question on the next open-weight launch with a technical report. The DeepSeek-V3 report attributed 2.788M H800 GPU-hours to official training and modeled roughly $5.576M using its assumed rental rate. Its figure excludes earlier research and ablation work, and it refers to V3. The R1 paper and repository give separate provenance for R1 and its licensing. [V3 technical report, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v2) · [R1 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) · [R1 repository](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1).

**Cut.** Put the footnote beside the headline number → define GPU-hour → show included runs and excluded work → distinguish training and serving → explain who gains access, what the license covers, and what is needed to reproduce the claim. Reprice only with a clearly identified date, hardware assumption, workload and source. [DeepSeek explainer reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mv4ZCiphwRU).

### 6. What the Sora shutdown teaches about relying on a hosted tool

**Status.** Evergreen until another relevant shutdown or platform decision. Use the official April 26, 2026 consumer web/app discontinuation and September 24, 2026 API discontinuation as separate points in the timeline. Make the consequences specific to creators or developers. A shutdown date documents the decision. Further claims about financing, usage, or motives need their own records. [OpenAI discontinuation notice](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152-what-to-know-about-the-sora-discontinuation) · [OpenAI API deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations).

**Cut.** Show the two dates → explain which projects depended on a hosted app versus an API → follow a hypothetical project through export or replacement using clearly labeled original visuals → compare which dependencies move with the project. The event-driven commentary is evidence of audience interest. Its reaction and sponsor beats are optional reference material for pacing. [Shutdown coverage reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZusUqY-x-Yc).

## Commissioning and measuring the first episodes

1. **Write the pitch and open before drafting.** Record the date, what changed, the viewer's question, the strongest source, beneficial or affected parties, and three discoveries. Draft two title/thumbnail pairs and a 30-second opening using the same evidence.
2. **Make an episode source pack.** Use the producer's existing `topic`, `sources` (title, URL and notes), and `clips` (title, direct URL, credit and optional trim) fields. In the notes, capture the exact claim, page or section, date, unit, denominator and caveat. Keep at least two accessible reporting sources and a decodable, reusable direct MP4 with its landing page and credit. The briefs above are reporting leads. They still need footage selection and clearance before production. [Producer schema](editor.mjs) · [Existing source pack example](pilot.json).
3. **Review the delivered promise.** Check the rendered opening for title/thumbnail match, first exhibit legibility and clear attribution. Reconcile every numerical comparison with its underlying source. Watch the whole cut at normal speed and check that each chapter changes the viewer's understanding. Treat old archive material as dated context.
4. **Record results at comparable ages.** Capture impressions, click-through rate by traffic source, 30-second retention, average view duration and percentage viewed, and views at 24 hours, 7 days and 28 days. Retain title/thumbnail versions and any changes. Compare nearby uploads of similar length and promise at the same elapsed age. High impressions with low click-through suggests testing packaging. Strong clicks with a steep early drop suggests inspecting the open for a promise mismatch. Check traffic mix and sample size before drawing either conclusion. [YouTube retention help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415) · [YouTube A/B testing help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400).

## Research method and scope

- Twelve Luna research lanes ran Monid searches across model launches, open models, chips, infrastructure, coding tools, labor, synthetic media, autonomy, platforms, large editorial channels, smaller channels, and recent news. The publishing-channel sample came from targeted follow-ups including `AI layoffs`, `OpenAI Sora shutdown`, `AI news CNBC WSJ`, `Bloomberg AI technology`, and topic/channel searches. Narrow short queries surfaced relevant videos that longer compound queries missed.
- Candidate discovery used Monid's YouTube search with the provider's 3–20-minute band, then screened for the inclusive 10:00–15:00 range, topical fit and clear editorial purpose. Duplicates were joined by video ID. Monid's Litescrape and DataForSEO detail endpoints supplied channel, duration, date, and views. Some search and detail runtimes differed by one second. The table uses details where available. Adjacent formats are labeled so tutorials, corporate films, and talk segments can be interpreted in context.
- Structure notes derive from Monid's SerpApi YouTube captions and timestamped chapters, or the uploader's description when captions were missing. Thumbnail observations come from inspecting five public preview images. These are packaging and narration observations. Shot-level pacing, CTR, paid distribution, private retention and historical view curves were outside the observable evidence.
- The results represent a targeted popularity and relevance sample, with observable demand skewing toward power, compute and employment. Some research lanes surfaced few direct matches. An all-time YouTube ranking and the causes of a video's view performance cannot be established from public metadata. For recurring scouting, log new candidates on discovery and revisit public counts at matched ages. Use the channel’s own Studio data to test whether the observed patterns transfer.
