# Hedgy showcase reel: The ticket that took off

## Topic and hook

**Mechanism:** a short squeeze. A short seller borrows shares, sells them, and later has to buy shares back to return the loan. If the price rises, the short seller loses money and may have to add collateral or close the position. Those buybacks add demand, which can push the price higher and pressure other short sellers to buy too.

**Why it hooks:** “Hedgy sells a concert ticket he borrowed” is an instant contradiction with a clear human stake. The ticket market makes borrowing, scarcity, the forced buyback, and the feedback loop visible without starting with finance jargon. A reunion announcement gives the price spike a vivid cause; the escalating buyback turns it into a set-piece.

## Production lock

- **Runtime:** 32.3 seconds, 14 shots. Narration is 98 words, one continuous paragraph, voiced by an energetic male narrator at about 1.15×.
- **Frame:** 1080 × 1920. Compose every shot edge-to-edge across x=0–1080 and y=0–1290, with foreground, middle distance, and deep background plus camera travel. Keep essential action and type within y=0–1260 for the renderer’s 60 px safety gap; player captions occupy y=1320–1650.
- **Look:** black, white, and greys; Hedgy is a naturally brown, flat 2D pixel sprite inside the shaded 3D voxel sets. Red appears only on the rising price path. No green or decorative accent colour. Use no more than one brief diegetic kinetic label at a time; it belongs on a sign, ticket, ledger, or stage structure, never as a floating headline.
- **Cut rhythm:** within each shot, add a small physical change every 0.4–0.8 seconds. Let the narration stay continuous across cuts. Sync words below are exact words or phrases in the paragraph; land the named action on that spoken event.
- **Mix:** close, energetic narration over a low noir pulse (muted kick, ticking metal, restrained sub). Duck the bed under every line. Build urgency after “reunion”; pull the bed down for a breath before “buy”; let the final bass hit land on “squeeze.” Sound cues below are relative to each shot’s start.

## Narration

Hedgy sells a concert ticket he borrowed. That’s short selling: he promises to return one, betting he can buy it back cheaper. His sale money stays locked with the broker as collateral while he waits for hype to fade. Then the band announces a reunion, and fans chase the few tickets left. As the price climbs, Hedgy’s losses grow; his broker asks for more collateral. He can’t cover it, so he must buy a ticket back now. That rush adds buyers, lifts prices and can scare other short sellers into covering. That feedback loop is a short squeeze.

## Three set-pieces

1. **Borrowed-ticket hook (shot 1):** Make the ticket feel dangerously close: coarse paper pixels rush into lens, then the camera dives through its perforation into a multi-storey venue. Hedgy must visibly sell the still-tethered ticket in the first second. Layer the ticket in extreme foreground, Hedgy at the counter, and balcony/city silhouettes behind; land the paper slap exactly on “sells.”
2. **Reunion-to-scarcity surge (shots 6–8):** Let the announcement physically unfold the venue, then pour a crowd through its switchback ramps while ticket windows close one by one. Carry the camera through that crowd and onto the first red price path only when “price climbs” begins. Build scale from a small ticket to an arena-wide rush, with no extra explanatory labels.
3. **Buyback feedback rocket (shots 11–14):** Show Hedgy’s buyback consume one of the last tickets, then turn the ticket queue into an accelerating demand loop. Each buyback lifts the red path; the rising line can trigger another short seller to cover. Ride the path through booths, spiral around the tower, and let the paper press create the “SHORT SQUEEZE” payoff. End with the line curving into the opening ticket for a seamless replay.

## Mechanism reference

The analogy is illustrative; the ticket itself is not meant to model every ticket-borrowing market. The finance beats follow the SEC and Investor.gov description: short sellers borrow and sell shares, later buy shares to return them, and may face margin requirements as prices rise. Sale proceeds are used as collateral for the borrowed shares.

- [Investor.gov: Stock Purchases and Sales, Long and Short](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/how-stock-markets-work/stock-purchases-and-sales-long-and)
- [SEC: Key Points About Regulation SHO](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)
- [SEC discussion of short-sale proceeds and collateral](https://www.sec.gov/dera/reportspubs/special-studies/short-sale-position-and-transaction-reporting.pdf)
