# PATCH NOTES

Brand identity · Version 1.2 · Updated 11 November 2026

**The world, updated.**

PATCH NOTES is an Instagram-first AI news publication. It makes AI releases, striking demos, tools, research and industry shifts understandable through Reels, carousels and static posts. Visual explainers are a major part of the Reel direction: show how the technology works as well as what just happened.

The feeling: a fast-moving technology publication with a black-and-white pixel-art identity. White pixel titles, the pixel wordmark, square details and occasional dithered artwork make PATCH NOTES recognizable. Clear reporting and purposeful visuals carry the story.

## How to use this guide

**These are recommendations with creative freedom.** The examples communicate the style; they do not prescribe every post's layout. Framing, pacing, composition, title placement, sequence, imagery and duration can change whenever another approach tells the story better.

Use pixel art for titles, labels, tags, numerals and details; captions/subtitles are bold clean sans in white on dark (black on light), never pixel type, with only the key word accented. Use a black, white and grey base with exactly one aggressive accent per post, default red `#FF2B2B` (alternates: lime `#B8FF1A`, orange `#FF5A00`, blue `#2F6BFF`, magenta `#FF1F8E`, yellow `#FFE600`). Reserve it for the most important element; never use it as a background tint, gradient or second hue. Code-animated Reels made with the shared motion library are the primary Reel method, with fast scene changes every 1.5–4 seconds and constant motion. Generated footage suits stories needing real-world imagery.

## Audience and positioning

For creators, builders, students, founders and technology-curious professionals who want to follow AI without reading every paper or announcement.

Their questions are: What happened? Can it actually do that? How does it work? Can I use it? Why should I care?

**Positioning:** the visual briefing for a world being updated by AI.

The name borrows the idea of software release notes: something changed, here is what is new, here is why you care. Pixel headings, short labels, square tags, stepped arrows, block cursors and dithered illustrations express that idea. Choose the details that suit a story; there is no requirement to use all of them.

## Voice and writing

Sound like a well-informed editor sharing something interesting and making it understandable. Curious, confident, concise and energetic. Build intrigue around something concrete, then pay it off.

Recommended writing habits:

- Lead with a development, a capability, a result or an interesting question.
- Keep cover titles short, often 6–12 words. Pixel type is wide; change the wording or arrangement before shrinking it.
- Use uppercase pixel lettering for authored titles and on-screen hooks. Use bold clean sans captions/subtitles, white on dark and black on light; never pixel type. Accent only the key word.
- For explainers, write around a visual sequence. Each meaningful step should add understanding rather than decorate the same point.
- Match the language to the evidence: an announcement, a demonstration and an available product are different things.
- Finish on the useful payoff or one natural CTA.

Possible headline structures, to fill only from verified reporting:

- `[COMPANY] JUST RELEASED [MODEL]. HERE'S WHAT CHANGED.`
- `WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU [ACTION]?`
- `HOW DOES [CAPABILITY] ACTUALLY WORK?`
- `THIS AI CAN [TASK]. WATCH THE RESULT.`
- `[NUMBER] AI UPDATES WORTH KNOWING THIS WEEK.`

For news captions, a concrete opening, a few short paragraphs, the important availability or limitation, and a source credit are a useful starting structure. For an explainer, a brief summary plus sources may be enough.

Useful CTAs include `Follow PATCH NOTES for the next update.`, `Save the breakdown.` or `Send this to someone building with AI.` A strong takeaway can end a post without a CTA.

Keep rumors attributed and verify dates, prices, benchmarks and availability. Never invent tests, statistics, quotations or firsthand experience. Treat explanatory animation as illustration, not footage proving a real capability. Drama in the headline should be supported by the reporting.

## Format recommendations

A starting publishing mix is **70% Reels, 20% carousels and 10% static posts** by post count. Adjust it as production and audience evidence develop. This is a recommendation, not the observed split of either reference account.

Within Reels, use code animation from the shared motion library as the primary method, with fast scene changes every 1.5–4 seconds and constant motion. Prioritize visual mechanism explainers guided by speedy_devv. Use generated footage when a story needs real-world imagery.

### Reels

**Visual explainer — major creative direction.** Begin with a concrete curiosity, surprising result or question. Reveal the mechanism through animated objects, diagrams, cutaways or comparisons. A 30–90 second range is a useful starting point; shorter and longer explanations are welcome when the story earns them. Vary framing and layout as the explanation develops.

**Demonstration — one possible direction.** Show a real capability and explain the context. A pixel title above a framed clip is one style study, not a mandatory arrangement. Full-screen media, commentary or a small corner title may work better. A simple result may suit 10–25 seconds.

**News brief — another possible direction.** A meaningful opening, the development, evidence and implication. Around 20–35 seconds is a starting range. Footage, source excerpts, animation, commentary and different sequences are all options.

**Weekly patch — optional roundup.** A small number of connected developments can make a useful briefing. Choose the length and rhythm for the material.

These structures and durations suggest possibilities. They should guide ideas while allowing creative freedom. A Reel can look different from every example and still feel like PATCH NOTES.

### Carousels and static posts

One possible carousel sequence is the headline, what changed, an example, practical meaning, access or limitation, and a takeaway. Around 5–7 slides can work, but change the sequence, layout and slide count freely. Give each slide a purpose.

A static post can use one strong image, source excerpt, statement or useful number with context in the caption. Use a sequence only when the story benefits from it.

## First content-engine test

A possible pilot is 20 posts: 14 Reels, 4 carousels and 2 static posts. Give visual explainers a substantial share of the Reels and enough production effort to test the direction meaningfully. Adjust the mix when a story or production constraint calls for it.

Record each post's source, pillar, format, hook, visual mechanism, production inputs and corrections needed before publication. A video brief becomes a finished Reel only after its actual media, edit, captions and export are ready. An animated explainer may need a storyboard, diagrams and motion assets; a caption alone is not a produced video.

Compare available outcomes at a consistent age, such as seven days: reach or views, Reel watch time, saves, shares, profile visits and attributable follows where provided. Keep definitions and denominators consistent; mark missing values unknown. Review production effort as well as audience response. A small pilot provides an initial signal, not a guarantee of growth.
