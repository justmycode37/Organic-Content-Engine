# Mysteries of the Sky — research briefs

Researched 24 November 2026. These are topic, claim, and visual briefs, not finished scripts. Sources below are original research or the relevant mission/observatory team's own material. Historical findings are dated; they should not be framed as breaking news.

Suggested first choices: **Refsdal** for a surprising prediction with a visible payoff; **WASP-39 b** for a simple scientific detective story; **S2** for an intuitive connection between motion and an invisible mass. This is an editorial judgment, not a forecast of audience performance.

## 1. How did astronomers predict a supernova's reappearance?

- **Claim:** Hubble recorded four lensed images of supernova Refsdal in November 2014. A further image was detected on 11 December 2015 at a predicted location. One explosion reached us along different gravitationally bent light paths, with different travel times. Researchers predicted a delayed image, not the star's original explosion. [Kelly et al., *ApJL* (2016), abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04654); [Hubble mission account and observation panels (2015)](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/hubble-captures-first-ever-predicted-exploding-star/).
- **Visual:** One flash behind a foreground galaxy cluster → several bent paths to the same observer → separate arrival clocks → the actual 2014 and 2015 Hubble panels, with one source identity maintained throughout.
- **Boundary:** Do not show the star exploding again or light moving at different speeds. The 2015 observation date is not an exact onset time. Lens models had different predictions; some matched better than others. Any simplified paths need the label “schematic.”

## 2. How can we identify a planet's atmosphere without visiting it?

- **Claim:** During a transit, a small portion of the star's light passes through the planet's atmosphere. Different gases absorb particular wavelengths. JWST's 2022 WASP-39 b spectrum showed a strong carbon-dioxide feature around **4.3 micrometres**. This is an inference from wavelength-dependent absorption, not a resolved photograph of gas. [JWST ERS team, *Identification of carbon dioxide in an exoplanet atmosphere* (2022), abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11692); [NASA's measured transmission spectrum and explanation (2022)](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/exoplanet-wasp-39-b-nirspec-transmission-spectrum/).
- **Visual:** A transit → magnify the thin atmospheric rim → compare incoming and transmitted light → reveal the measured 4.3 μm feature. Use the actual spectrum as the final evidence.
- **Boundary:** Infrared wavelengths are invisible to human eyes; any visible palette is explanatory. The published transmission graph plots **light blocked**, so stronger absorption makes a peak. CO₂ alone does not establish life. Do not turn this hot gas giant into an Earth analogue.

## 3. How do you weigh an object you cannot see?

- **Claim:** Astronomers tracked stars orbiting Sagittarius A*. S2 completes an orbit in about **16 years**. Its orbit and speed constrain an extremely compact central mass of roughly **four million Suns**; precise observations also detected the forward turning of its orbit predicted by general relativity. [ESO/GRAVITY observation report (2020)](https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2006/); [GRAVITY Collaboration, *Detection of the Schwarzschild precession in the orbit of the star S2* (2020)](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2020/04/aa37813-20.pdf).
- **Visual:** Successive measured star positions → a fitted orbit around a marked, unseen centre → faster motion near closest approach → infer the central mass. Use the source's observed-position evidence before introducing a clean model.
- **Boundary:** Orbital sizes and times require a distance estimate; a pretty ellipse alone does not weigh the centre. Label accelerated playback. S2 does not touch the horizon. If including the rosette, label any exaggerated precession: the real shift per orbit is small. Avoid “fastest known star” superlatives.

## 4. Why does a black hole's flat disk appear to bend above it?

- **Claim:** In a nearly edge-on view, gravity redirects light from an accretion disk so parts normally hidden behind the black hole become visible. The apparent arcs need not be gas physically bent into those shapes. NASA's relativistic visualization demonstrates how the appearance changes with viewing angle. [NASA/Goddard explanation (2019)](https://www.nasa.gov/universe/nasa-visualization-shows-a-black-holes-warped-world/); [scientist-created visualization and corrected viewing-angle sequence](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/13326/).
- **Visual:** Establish a flat disk in an explicitly schematic geometry view → change to the observer's lensed view → track light from a marked far-side patch into an apparent arc → compare with the NASA simulation.
- **Boundary:** Label the result “simulation”; not all black holes have luminous disks. Do not animate the disk itself folding upward. Do not equate every bright ring with a narrowly defined photon ring, or the dark region with an exposed solid surface. [Gralla, Holz & Wald, *Black Hole Shadows, Photon Rings, and Lensing Rings* (2019), abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00873).

## 5. How did a collision of invisible black holes move our detectors?

- **Claim:** On **14 September 2015**, both LIGO detectors recorded GW150914. Its increasing-frequency signal matched the inspiral, merger, and settling of two black holes. The measured peak strain was about **10⁻²¹**. The observation was announced in February 2016. [LIGO/Virgo discovery paper (2016), abstract and Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03837); [collaboration's event page](https://ligo.org/detections/gw150914/).
- **Visual:** A labelled merger simulation → a ring of free test masses alternately stretches and squeezes → an L-shaped interferometer diagram → the actual two-detector waveforms. Pair the increasing orbital rate with the increasingly close waveform cycles.
- **Boundary:** The deformation must be hugely exaggerated and labelled. Strain is a fractional distortion, not a distance. The plotted signal is not a telescope image; any audible chirp is a rendering of the measured waveform, not sound travelling through interstellar vacuum. Keep the interferometer schematic separate from literal apparatus motion.

## 6. What happens when a cluster's gas and its inferred mass part company?

- **Claim:** In the Bullet Cluster, X-ray observations locate hot gas containing most of the ordinary matter. Gravitational lensing maps place the main mass concentrations elsewhere, roughly with the galaxies. This separation is strong evidence for an additional unseen mass component. The observation does not identify the dark-matter particle. [Clowe et al., *ApJL* (2006), abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0608407); [Chandra's observation composite, layer descriptions, and credits (2006)](https://chandra.harvard.edu/photo/2006/1e0657/index.html).
- **Visual:** Reveal three aligned layers separately: optical galaxies → X-ray gas → lensing-inferred mass contours. Only then show a labelled schematic cluster collision to explain why collisional gas can lag behind.
- **Boundary:** The familiar blue region is an **inferred mass map**, not photographed glowing dark matter. Color is assigned to different datasets. Most ordinary cluster matter is gas, not the visible stars. Do not claim this single image measures a particle's identity or settles every alternative theory of gravity.

## 7. Is a pulsar really switching on and off?

- **Claim:** For the thermal X-ray pulsars studied by NICER, brightness changes as rotating surface hot spots change their orientation to us. Strong gravity also redirects some far-side light into view. The pulse profile therefore carries information about the star's compactness. [NICER team's light-curve explanation and measured J0030 profile (2017)](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/nicer/science_nuggets/20171006.html).
- **Visual:** A generic rotating neutron star with an identified hot region → fixed observer sightline → brightness trace synchronized to rotation → introduce light bending as the reason a far-side region can remain partly visible.
- **Boundary:** Keep this scoped to thermal X-ray pulsations; pulsar emission has other components. Do not show repeated explosions, a star-wide flashing bulb, or rapid full-screen strobing. If naming J0030, do not present two perfect opposite polar spots as its measured map: NICER's 2019 models found more complex spot arrangements. Those maps are model reconstructions. [NASA's NICER model visualizations (2019)](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/13240).

## 8. How can the Sun's outer atmosphere be hotter than its visible surface?

- **Claim:** The visible surface is around **6,000°C**, while coronal plasma reaches around **a million degrees**. Energy transfer through the magnetized plasma is central to investigating the difference. A 2023 study combined Solar Orbiter remote observations and Parker Solar Probe local measurements to estimate heating in a particular slow-wind region. [ESA mission account (2023)](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Solar_Orbiter/Solar_Orbiter_closes_in_on_the_solution_to_a_65-year-old_solar_mystery); [Telloni et al., *Coronal Heating Rate in the Slow Solar Wind* (2023), abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10819).
- **Visual:** A labelled temperature profile from photosphere into corona → magnetic and plasma fluctuations transferring energy to smaller scales → the two spacecraft sampling the corresponding plasma region. Keep the mechanism panel explicitly schematic.
- **Boundary:** Treat this as an investigation, not a “mystery solved” reveal. The cited study sampled roughly **6.3–13.3 solar radii**, not every coronal environment, and its estimate has model assumptions. High temperature does not imply a dense ocean of heat. Refresh the research before making claims about which mechanisms dominate today.
