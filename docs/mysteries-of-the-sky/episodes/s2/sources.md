# The Star That Weighed a Black Hole

## Science

- The GRAVITY collaboration measured the combined gravitational redshift and transverse Doppler contribution around S2’s May 2018 passage. The measured shift corresponds to roughly 200 km/s. The paper gives a closest-approach distance near 120 AU and a speed around 7,650 km/s. [2018 observation and methods](https://www.eso.org/public/archives/releases/sciencepapers/eso1825/eso1825b.pdf)
- The 2020 orbital fit detected a prograde advance of about 12 arcminutes per orbit. The analysis discusses reference-frame calibration, blended sources, and the role of the incoming and outgoing branches. [Precession measurement](https://www.eso.org/public/archives/releases/sciencepapers/eso2006/eso2006a.pdf)
- Subsequent orbit fits constrained the central mass and distance and improved the precision of the precession measurement. [2021 Galactic-centre orbit analysis](https://www.eso.org/public/archives/releases/sciencepapers/eso2119/eso2119b.pdf)

## Observations and illustrations

- The wide-field journey toward Sagittarius A* combines observations at progressively smaller scales. [ESO/GRAVITY Collaboration zoom](https://www.eso.org/public/videos/eso1825c/)
- The dated GRAVITY sequence shows S2 near pericentre. The infrared time-lapse shows multiple moving stars. [Close approach](https://www.eso.org/public/videos/eso1825d/) · [ESO/MPE infrared observations](https://www.eso.org/public/videos/eso1825e/)
- Rendered orbit views and the exaggerated precession sequence are artist’s animations. [ESO/L. Calçada/SpaceEngine orbit](https://www.eso.org/public/videos/eso1825g/) · [ESO/L. Calçada precession](https://www.eso.org/public/videos/eso2006b/)

The original diagrams solve Kepler’s equation to space positions at equal time intervals. The projected spectra, wavefronts, redshift residual, and expanded precession angle are explanatory illustrations. Source footage is used without its original audio.
