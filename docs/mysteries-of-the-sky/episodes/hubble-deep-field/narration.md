# The Sky That Looked Empty

## 01 · The small square

That little square looked almost empty. Hubble pointed at it for ten days. The final picture held about three thousand galaxies. How do you go from a dark patch to a picture full of galaxies without pointing the telescope anywhere else?

## 02 · The same patch

In December nineteen ninety-five, Hubble took hundreds of exposures of the same patch near the Big Dipper. Each exposure caught some light from faint galaxies. It also picked up fluctuations in the background and the camera. In a single frame, those fluctuations can hide a faint galaxy.

## 03 · What stays in place

Line up the exposures so the same galaxy falls in the same place. Its light keeps adding up in that spot. The random fluctuations change from frame to frame. Average the aligned exposures and the galaxy holds its brightness while much of the noise fades. Now you can make out a shape that was hard to see in any one frame.

## 04 · Thousands of galaxies

Hubble took three hundred and forty-two exposures through four filters. The team used them to build the Deep Field. This is the real image. Look at how many different shapes fit inside that tiny patch. Their light took different amounts of time to reach us, so the picture holds galaxies from different chapters of cosmic history.
