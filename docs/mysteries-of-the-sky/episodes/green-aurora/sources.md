# The Light Oxygen Makes — source ledger

Chapter numbers follow the narration's playback order. The schematic notes cover the existing animation and the illustrations proposed for the long-form chapters. Wavelengths use the conventional rounded air labels for the lines. Heights are typical values or measured ranges for the stated observations.

## 01 · Why green?

- **Claims.** Atomic oxygen produces the dominant visible line in most auroras, centered near 557.7 nm. NASA's Expedition 6 account confirms astronaut observations of green curtains and limb profiles from the ISS. It describes favorable orbital passes and gives no basis for an every-night claim. The first reported wavelength and the laboratory identification are about 57–58 years apart. See chapters 02–03. [Humberset et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JA024405) · [NASA Expedition 6 account](https://eol.jsc.nasa.gov/Collections/EarthObservatory/articles/AuroraDancingintheNight.htm)
- **Schematic visuals.** Opening globe, green ribbon, and isolated spectral line. The long-form opening uses a portrait cutout and a mosaic of cropped, graded aurora photos. The mosaic is illustrative, and its embedded photos are observations.

## 02 · A line without a name

- **Claims.** In his 1868 report, Ångström describes observations in winter 1867–68 and records that he had communicated the observation verbally in autumn 1867. He measured the bright line at 5567 Å and saw much fainter bands. That early wavelength differs from the modern value. [Ångström, *Recherches sur le spectre solaire*, p. 42](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a9/Recherches_sur_le_spectre_solaire_%28IA_recherchessurle00nggoog%29.pdf)
- **Claims.** A spectroscope separates wavelengths. Hot incandescent matter can yield a continuum, while a thin excited atomic gas produces characteristic emission lines. Researchers proposed competing identifications, including an unknown element nicknamed geocoronium and a nitrogen hypothesis. Some laboratory lines came close without matching the measured wavelength. The historical evidence supports a long-running puzzle over the identification. [NASA Webb spectroscopy](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/science-overview/science-explainers/spectroscopy-101-types-of-spectra-and-spectroscopy/) · [Royal Society meeting report, *Nature*, 1924](https://www.nature.com/articles/114030b0) · [Royal Astronomical Society presidential address, 1945](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1945MNRAS.105..138M)
- **Schematic visuals.** Prism, continuum, emission barcodes, and historical spectroscope. Use a facsimile or explicitly identify any reconstructed historical spectrum.

## 03 · The laboratory answer

- **Claims.** John Cunningham McLennan and Gordon Merritt Shrum reproduced the line in Toronto in 1925 with an electrical discharge in oxygen with a large admixture of helium. The experiment identified oxygen. The atomic energy-level assignment followed in 1927. The specific role of helium's quenching efficiency remains unverified. Special laboratory conditions can produce the line. [McLennan and Shrum, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 108, 501–512](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1925.0088) · [Royal Society meeting report, *Nature*, 1925](https://www.nature.com/articles/115998b0) · [Royal Astronomical Society account of Hopfield's assignment](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1945MNRAS.105..138M)
- **Schematic visuals.** Discharge tube, electrodes, gas, and spectral comparison. Any apparatus layout is a reconstruction.

## 04 · Where the electrons come from

- **Claims.** The solar wind supplies energy that couples into Earth's magnetosphere. The nightside field forms a magnetotail, where energy can build up and be released. For the nightside pathway shown, the electrons that precipitate are magnetospheric. Acceleration and motion along field lines lead to atmospheric collisions in ovals around both magnetic poles. [NOAA SWPC Aurora Tutorial](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/content/aurora-tutorial) · [NASA aurora science](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/auroras/) · [Frey et al. 2010, simultaneous ground and THEMIS substorm measurements](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JA015537)
- **Schematic visuals.** Solar wind, magnetopause, tail, field lines, moving electron, and ovals. The analytic dipole in the current animation represents field-guided travel. A separate distorted field drawing would represent the nightside tail.

## 14 · When the oval grows

- **Claims.** Fast solar wind and coronal mass ejections can bring stronger coupling, especially with a southward interplanetary field, and the auroral oval can expand equatorward. The May 10–11, 2024 G5 event was the strongest geomagnetic storm in roughly two decades and brought aurora to unusually low latitudes. Observer reports and photographs included abundant red and pink. [NOAA SWPC Aurora Tutorial](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/content/aurora-tutorial) · [NASA storm summary](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/historic-geomagnetic-storm-dazzles-152815/) · [Gannon storm citizen-science study, §§4.1.2–4.1.3](https://gc.copernicus.org/articles/7/297/2024/)
- **Claims.** From a sufficiently distant sightline, Earth's curvature can hide lower green emission while higher red remains visible. This is a geometric inference from the different emission heights and is consistent with reported poleward-horizon observations. Soft electron precipitation can also favor high red oxygen emission. Some pink or purple came from nitrogen emissions, with possible sunlight scattering at high altitude, and some extreme low-latitude red reports may involve airglow or stable auroral red arcs. [NOAA viewing geometry](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/content/tips-viewing-aurora) · [Gillies et al. 2017, red emission heights](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JA023758) · [Gannon storm citizen-science study](https://gc.copernicus.org/articles/7/297/2024/) · [Hayakawa et al. 2025, storm reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07665)
- **Schematic visuals.** Expanded ovals and a curved-Earth cross-section with observer and tangent sightline. Layer heights and Earth radius can be exaggerated for visibility. The cross-section illustrates one viewing geometry.

## 05 · One collision

- **Claims.** A few keV is representative for many visible auroras. The incoming electrons lose energy through successive collisions, with the depth of deposition depending on energy. About 114 km was the typical *peak emission height* for green and blue in a Lapland survey. Collisions begin higher and occur across a range of altitudes. The air there contains molecular nitrogen, molecular oxygen, and abundant atomic oxygen produced by solar UV. [Fang et al. 2010, electron-impact altitude profiles](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010GL045406) · [Whiter et al. 2023, measured heights, model and atmospheric species](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [Zhu et al. 2015, mesospheric atomic oxygen](https://doi.org/10.1029/2015JA021405)
- **Claims.** Direct impacts by primary or secondary electrons can excite O(¹S). Transfer from excited N₂ and other pathways also contribute and can dominate under some conditions. The excited atom can lose energy by radiation or through collisions. [Whiter et al. 2023, §4](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023)
- **Schematic visuals.** One representative impact, the secondary electron, and the excited atom. Particle sizes, trajectories, and timing are illustrative.

## 21 · Rowan · the delivery

- **Claims.** The electron supplies energy, and light from this line comes from the excited oxygen atom's later radiative transition. That distinction is sound in the context of this line. [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [Humberset et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JA024405)
- **Schematic visuals.** Any electron marker or trail is a visual aid. Reserve the visible green photon for the atomic emission.

## 06 · A fixed amount of energy

- **Claims.** Atoms have discrete energy levels. In a single-photon radiative transition a larger energy gap means shorter wavelength. The green O I line comes from ¹S₀ → ¹D₂, about 2.22 eV, conventionally labeled 557.7 nm (about 557.9 nm in vacuum). The transition sets the line center. Thermal motion and other effects give a spectral line finite width, and apparent color can vary with other emissions and camera response. Electron energy affects excitation rates and height. [NIST Atomic Spectra Database, O I lines](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/lines_form.html) · [NIST CODATA constants](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wallet_2022.pdf) · [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023)
- **Schematic visuals.** Energy ladder, stylized atom, level change, photon wave, and comparison colors. The atom graphic represents a change of internal state.

## 15 · Reading the line

- **Claims.** A spectrograph disperses light and a calibrated detector measures line positions. A green aurora often has a strong, narrow 557.7 nm line alongside other emissions. The calculation E = hc/λ gives about 2.22 eV using the rounded wavelength. Brightness and curtain shape do not determine the atomic gap. [NASA Hubble spectroscopy](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-spectroscopy/) · [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [NIST CODATA constants](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wallet_2022.pdf)
- **Schematic visuals.** Instrument, detector stripe, rainbow scale, and energy calculation. Line intensity and width are illustrative.

## 07 · The slow atom

- **Claims.** Many allowed atomic transitions have radiative lifetimes on the order of nanoseconds. The metastable O(¹S) level has a radiative lifetime of around 0.75 s. That is an average in the absence of quenching, with individual decays spread over time. The usual electric-dipole transition is forbidden by selection rules, while weaker multipole channels are allowed. [NIST ASD, transition probabilities and multipole notation](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/Html/lineshelp.html) · [Humberset et al. 2018, §5.1.3](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JA024405)
- **Schematic visuals.** Contrasting decay times and a representative delayed flash. Avoid synchronizing all green emissions at a fixed delay.

## 08 · Why the lab missed it

- **Claims.** Air molecules at room conditions collide on the order of billions of times per second. A suitable collision can remove the oxygen atom's excitation before it radiates. Competition between collisional and radiative loss depends on density, collision partners, and rates. The historical oxygen–helium mixture produced the line, and the specific role of helium's quenching efficiency remains unverified. [Harvard kinetic-theory notes](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/schwartz/files/2-diffusion.pdf) · [Whiter et al. 2023, §4](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [McLennan and Shrum 1925](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1925.0088)
- **Schematic visuals.** Crowded and rarefied gases, one quenching interaction, one radiative decay, and the comparison with a laboratory tube. Collision rates and sizes are illustrative.

## 09 · Height sets the color

- **Claims.** Quenching suppresses 557.7 nm emission as density increases, often appreciably below about 100 km, helping create a sharp lower border. This is a gradual, condition-dependent effect. Green-line emission can occasionally be measured below 100 km. [Humberset et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JA024405) · [Lee et al. 2017, measured summer polar mesospheric green emission](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JA023413)
- **Claims.** O(¹D), the lower level of the green transition, produces the 630.0 nm red line when it decays to ³P₂. Its lifetime is roughly two minutes. Red-line aurora is often found around 200 km or higher, with a broad reported range of roughly 150 to over 400 km and significant quenching at lower heights. The layered curtain is a common pattern. Excitation, including electron energy, and quenching both shape the altitude profiles. [Stoffregen and Omholt 1960, observed red-line decay](https://doi.org/10.1038/185028a0) · [Solomon, Hays and Abreu 1988](https://doi.org/10.1029/JA093iA09p09867) · [Gillies et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JA023758)
- **Schematic visuals.** Three oxygen levels, red and green profiles, and the sharp green lower edge. Use overlapping layers and a height scale marked as approximate.

## 22 · Rowan · reading the height

- **Claims.** Rowan's rough color guide works for a layered curtain. A pink or purple *bottom fringe* can mark prompt molecular emissions where more energetic precipitation penetrates deeper. Color alone does not give an altitude, and the blue 427.8 nm band often peaks near the green line. [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [Hallinan et al. 1998, lower-border spectra](https://doi.org/10.1029/97JA03459) · [Gillies et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JA023758)
- **Schematic visuals.** Approximate height labels and fringe highlighting. The lower border is the example, with no universal color-to-height mapping.

## 10 · The other colors

- **Claims.** Excited molecular nitrogen ions emit blue-violet bands including the 427.8 nm first-negative band. Neutral N₂ first-positive bands can supply red to a low pink or crimson fringe, along with blue molecular emissions. These emissions can respond promptly to local excitation. The lower-fringe mechanism is supported by spectra and particle observations. [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [Hallinan et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1029/97JA03459) · [Optical and particle observations of type B red aurora](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009GL041037)
- **Claims.** Dark-adapted rods have peak sensitivity near 507 nm (blue-green) and do not distinguish colors. At intermediate brightness, cones can still give a green impression. Long-exposure cameras can reveal colors too faint for the observer to distinguish. [CIE scotopic sensitivity data](https://www.cie.co.at/datatable/cie-spectral-luminous-efficiency-scotopic-vision) · [Gannon storm study, §4.2](https://gc.copernicus.org/articles/7/297/2024/)
- **Schematic visuals.** Colored fringe, nitrogen bands, and eye-versus-camera comparison. Apparent colors depend on brightness and the rendering.

## 11 · Why curtains

- **Claims.** Field-aligned precipitation can make thin, elongated structures. Maggs and Davis measured fine-scale optical widths down to about 70 m with a median around 230 m. Observations also report some long arcs extending thousands of kilometers. The optical width varies across arcs and along their length. Looking along a sheet integrates more light than looking across the same sheet. [Maggs and Davis 1968](https://doi.org/10.1016/0032-0633(68)90069-X) · [Frey et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JA015537) · [NASA Expedition 6 account](https://eol.jsc.nasa.gov/Collections/EarthObservatory/articles/AuroraDancingintheNight.htm)
- **Claims.** Green emission can vary. Its finite lifetime delays and smooths rapid changes, making fast flicker much clearer in prompt bands such as N₂⁺ 427.8 nm. [Humberset et al. 2018, measured variability and §5.1.3](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JA024405) · [Flickering-aurora observations](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GL072956)
- **Schematic visuals.** Folding ribbons, edge-on and broadside views, sparse emitting atoms, and prompt-versus-smoothed light curves. The drawing is a radiative geometry example.

## 12 · Seen from orbit

- **Claims.** The ISS views aurora from roughly 400 km and can see emission profiles edge-on along the limb. The green aurora can extend upward from near 100 km. A thin green line near the limb can be airglow, and a higher red layer is visible in some auroras. The presence and relative brightness of red vary by display. [NASA Expedition 6 account and photo captions](https://eol.jsc.nasa.gov/Collections/EarthObservatory/articles/AuroraDancingintheNight.htm) · [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023)
- **Schematic visuals.** Orbital approach, atmosphere and altitude overlays, and the animated transition to the credited photo. The astronaut photographs are observations. Crops, color grading, camera moves and overlays are editorial treatments.

## 23 · Rowan · color is not a spectrum

- **Claims.** Rowan's distinction is sound for the film's photograph. A broad-band RGB image shows color. A wavelength-resolved spectrum identifies the line. Perseverance's simultaneous imaging and spectroscopy also demonstrate the distinction. [NASA Hubble spectroscopy](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-spectroscopy/) · [Knutsen et al. 2025, Figs. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ads1563)
- **Schematic visuals.** Detector or spectrum shown beside the ISS image. Any drawn spectrum represents a separate spectroscopic observation or an illustration.

## 17 · The sky is never fully dark

- **Claims.** Solar UV splits O₂. In the two-step Barth mechanism, two oxygen atoms recombine with the help of a third body, producing excited O₂, which can transfer energy to an oxygen atom. That atom emits the same 557.7 nm line. The mesospheric green nightglow is typically around 90–100 km, with satellite retrievals sampling roughly 85–105 km. Dark-sky observers can sometimes see airglow, and limb viewing from orbit highlights the thin layer. Airglow brightness and coverage vary. [Barth and Hildebrandt 1961](https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ066i003p00985) · [Lednyts'kyy et al. 2015, Envisat limb measurements](https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-8-1021-2015) · [NASA ISS nightglow observations](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-awash-in-lights-of-the-night-92912/) · [National Park Service dark-sky guide](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/nightskies/natural-light-in-night-sky.htm)
- **Schematic visuals.** Daytime dissociation, nighttime recombination and energy transfer, and the green limb layer. Treat shell thickness, uniformity, and brightness as illustrative.

## 18 · Green on Mars

- **Claims.** The Martian atmosphere is mostly CO₂, with molecular oxygen a trace gas. CO₂ photodissociation supplies atmospheric atomic oxygen. Solar energetic particle precipitation excites Martian auroral emission. Knutsen et al. identify the emitted line and link it to the particle event. The emitting atoms' formation pathway during that storm remains unverified. [NASA Mars fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html) · [Gérard et al. 2020, ExoMars dayside oxygen observations](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-020-1123-2) · [Knutsen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ads1563)
- **Claims.** On March 18, 2024 at Jezero Crater, Perseverance's SuperCam detected the O I 557.7 nm line and Mastcam-Z captured the diffuse glow. The paper estimates about 101 rayleighs for the observed event versus a cited visual threshold around 250. It models about 200 rayleighs with less dust and suggests stronger events or better conditions could make it visible to astronauts. [Knutsen et al. 2025, results and discussion](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/331745) · [NASA mission report](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/nasa-observes-first-visible-light-auroras-at-mars/)
- **Schematic visuals.** Mars, rover, incoming particles, and reconstructed diffuse sky glow. Identify any licensed Mastcam-Z image or reproduction of the SuperCam spectrum as observation and credit separately. The detected emission was diffuse across the camera's field.

## 13 · Answer

- **Claims.** Green is often brightest because excitation of atomic oxygen and survival against collisional quenching overlap across an altitude range, with a typical green peak around 114 km in the cited survey. The oxygen is already present. The ¹S state radiates after about 0.75 s on average without quenching, emitting about 2.22 eV in the green branch. Higher up, lower density reduces quenching, and softer precipitation can favor the slower red line. [Whiter et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-41-1-2023) · [Humberset et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JA024405) · [Gillies et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JA023758) · [NIST CODATA constants](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wallet_2022.pdf)
- **Schematic visuals.** Single photon, many independent emitters, and pullback to a curtain. An arc can stretch a thousand kilometers, while the specific animated curtain has no measured extent. [Frey et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JA015537)
