# The Moon That Ran Late

- The 1676 report translated in the *Philosophical Transactions* describes Io entering and leaving Jupiter’s shadow, about 42½ hours between revolutions, and the increasing delay as Earth receded. It gives the recorded Nov. 9 Paris time of 5:35:45 in the evening, about ten minutes after a prediction based on the August observations. The episode uses the time as recorded in the published report. [Royal Society archive](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/calmview/Record.aspx?id=CLP%2F8i%2F2&src=CalmView.Catalog) · [Full text](https://jimclifford.ca/philosophical-transactions-ocr-1665-1869/articles/jstor-101779/)
- The visible dots in the eclipse illustration represent Io in or out of direct sunlight. Its actual orbit is perturbed by neighboring moons. The animation uses a circular orbit, enlarged bodies, compressed travel time, and an idealized Jupiter shadow and Earth trajectory to show the geometric delay. No per-eclipse data points are reconstructed. [NASA Io facts](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/io/facts/)
