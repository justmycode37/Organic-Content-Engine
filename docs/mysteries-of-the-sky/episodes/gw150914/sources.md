# The Chirp That Crossed Earth

- The discovery paper gives the date, strain interpretation and first event. Figure 1 shows filtered detector strain, the 6.9 ms Livingston-to-Hanford delay and the sign inversion for comparing the instruments. Figure 2 links the modeled waveform to the inspiral, merger and ringdown. [LIGO Scientific Collaboration and Virgo, discovery paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03837)
- Figure 1 data files supply the observed Hanford and Livingston strain series and the Hanford numerical-relativity comparison. The plotted data are scaled by 10²¹ and shown relative to 09:50:45 UTC. Data on screen are decimated from the collaboration’s 35–350 Hz visualization without changing the clock. Hanford is shifted and inverted only during the labeled alignment. [GWOSC event data and CC BY 4.0 credit](https://gwosc.org/events/GW150914/)
- The simplified instrument description follows the collaboration’s diagram. The animations omit optical cavities and control loops, exaggerate the distance changes and trace a plane gravitational wave in a face-on orientation. [LIGO science summary](https://ligo.org/science-summaries/GW150914/)
- The finale uses the GWOSC Livingston sonification with whitening and band-passing and a 400 Hz upward frequency shift. The audio is rendered from the public data, cropped around the chirp and mixed for audibility. [GWOSC sonification and acknowledgments](https://gwosc.org/events/GW150914/#audio-files)
