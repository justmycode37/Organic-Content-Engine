# How Saturn’s Rings Heard Its Core

## Evidence

- Matthew M. Hedman and Philip D. Nicholson, *Kronoseismology: Using Density Waves in Saturn’s C Ring to Probe the Planet’s Interior* (2013). Cassini VIMS stellar occultations constrain the rotation speeds and spiral symmetries of waves in the C ring. Inferred pattern speeds can match planetary oscillations, and the features are mostly away from strong known satellite resonances. [The Astronomical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/146/1/12)
- Christopher R. Mankovich and Jim Fuller, *A diffuse core in Saturn revealed by ring seismology* (2021). The innermost highlighted wave, W76.44, is consistent with a low-frequency gravity mode. Narrow transitions make that modeled mode too fast for the observed resonance. Gradients extending nearly to the surface predict slower rhythms for the wave. Jointly fitting specific wave frequencies and Cassini gravity data favors a stable transition out to about 0.6 Saturn radii. The exact composition gradient is model dependent. [Nature Astronomy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-021-01448-3) · [Open text and figure captions](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.13385)
- NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute, *Saturn’s Watch Spiral* (2008). Cassini image PIA09894 provides a visual reference for how tightly wound density waves look in the rings. It shows moon-driven waves in the A ring and belongs with the satellite explanation. [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/saturns-watch-spiral/)
- The globe and base ring maps come from Solar System Scope. Credit the CC BY 4.0 maps and adaptations in the rendered credits. [Texture credits](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/)

## Animation direction

1. Show Saturn on an oversized record-print collage. Give the paper, Saturn and the groove separate depth and motion. A stylus picks up a single lime trace. Push through the trace into the ring plane, with the groove becoming the actual radial scan guide. Identify the opening as an analogy.
2. Carry the marked radius through the star, the rings and the detector. Generate the brightness trace during the scan and preserve it while the next scan draws with shifted peaks. Label all fabricated readings illustrative. Color both traces consistently between the view from above and the detector.
3. Show repeat tugs on a few individually trackable particles, then widen to an orbiting field with coherent bunches. Keep one particle highlighted as a denser region moves past it. Show a moon-driven A-ring example as a separate piece of evidence, with its source visible.
4. Match the repeating pull of the moon to the changing pull of the two-lobed planetary mode. Cross the planet's surface into an interior material gradient. Follow a tracked parcel as it overshoots its equilibrium position and mark the direction of its restoring buoyancy. Exaggerate the mode and label it.
5. Keep the same measured wave guide as the sharp and diffuse cutaways change the modeled frequency. The figure must make a fixed observation and movable model distinguishable. Mark 0.6 from the center outward rather than emphasizing a hard boundary. The cutaways and model curves are schematic.
6. Return from the diffuse gradient to the first scan in one continuous pullback. Use the same marked groove to close on the opening collage and transition to the credits.
